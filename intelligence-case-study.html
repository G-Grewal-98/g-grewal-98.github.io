GURAMRIT GREWAL / PROJECT CASE STUDY

Procurement intelligence

A procurement analytics project combining supplier research, cost modelling, data validation and Power BI decision support. Interactive examples use fictional data.

The question

When several suppliers list apparently similar products, which comparisons are credible, what would a proposed quantity cost after delivery and VAT, and what evidence is still missing? The project connects product research, price evidence and purchasing economics without treating a low advertised price as approval to buy.

My retail experience at Vino Kinver provided the commercial context: supplier enquiries, product selection, stock monitoring and price comparisons. The technical continuation is a portfolio implementation. It is not evidence of realised savings or of a live automated procurement system operating in the shop.

Project scope and data integrity

The procurement project now covers 240+ products, building on an original workbook with 240 product records and associated purchasing decisions. The current extended historical snapshot contains 251 supplier offers, 246 product identities (including labelled synthetic test additions), five supplier identities and 395 retail observations. These are dated record counts, not live product availability, customer outcomes or sales results.

The legacy decision population contains 222 benchmarked and 18 unbenchmarked offers: 222 divided by 240 is 92.5%. Eleven of the 251 offers have no legacy decision. Using all offers as the coverage denominator would answer a different question.

How the system works

  1. Controlled exportsAuthorised CSV, TSV and values-only XLSX files use explicit supplier and column mappings.
  2. Listing registrySupplier-scoped aliases preserve identity. Valid GTIN and complete variant attributes can support exact matches; uncertain identities require review.
  3. Evidence and economicsRecorded retail evidence is classified separately from availability. Quantity rules calculate cost, margin, cash and restrictions using Decimal arithmetic.
  4. Committed outputValidated SQLite and CSV generations are published only after checks. Failed refreshes retain the previous generation.
  5. ReportingThe private Power BI report preserves its five original pages and adds QA validation, offer scenarios and recorded evidence views.

Commercial judgement encoded in the model

Product identity

Language, region, edition, pack size and variant remain distinguishable. A matching name is insufficient. Missing SKU values are not replaced with invented supplier identifiers.

Comparable costs

MOQ, order multiples, stock, minimum spend, delivery and VAT must be compatible before ranking. A mixed-product basket applies supplier charges at order level. Independent supplier orders are alternatives and must not be summed.

Evidence limits

Sold-out, stale or conflicting references remain visible. Historical prices are not live availability checks. Stock cover remains blank when verified demand is missing.

The private generation has 1,004 quantity-scenario rows and zero cost-eligible rows because supplier terms are unverified. This restriction is deliberate. The separate fictional fixture includes test terms and demand so successful and blocked examples can both be demonstrated safely.

Reliable data, reviewable decisions

Validation covers product identity, VAT-aware costs, minimum-order rules, retail evidence, duplicate imports and historical price changes. An invalid import cannot replace the previous successful data generation.

During Windows testing, a failed-import cleanup issue was found and corrected by closing SQLite connections before removing staging files. The refresh process now retains its last valid data when an import fails, providing a more reliable basis for reporting.

The Power BI report was compiled, saved, reopened and refreshed in Desktop. Its calculations were checked for accurate totals, filter behaviour, evidence exclusions and appropriate blanks when supplier information is insufficient. Detailed verification results remain available in the downloadable technical evidence.

Explore the analysis yourself

The interactive public example lets visitors change products, suppliers and quantities to explore how purchasing costs and eligibility change. The fictional price history demonstrates tracking a supplier price reduction without presenting it as a real negotiated saving.

A separate fictional margin model on the main page shows how target margins, estimated demand and cash commitments influence an outcome. Both demonstrations are illustrative rather than live purchasing tools.

Next: authorised supplier integrations

The next planned step is to connect permitted supplier catalogues, authorised website or API access, and wholesale email attachments. Verified commercial terms and reliable inventory and sales records will be needed to support genuine buying recommendations. The system will remain a human-reviewed decision-support tool rather than placing orders automatically.
