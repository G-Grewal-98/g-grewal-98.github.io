01 / FEATURED PROJECT · REAL WORK

Retail Procurement & Pricing Analytics — Project Case Study

Turning a wholesale catalogue and fragmented retail listings into a reviewable purchasing decision.

The business question

Vino Kinver is a convenience store selling alcohol, confectionery and everyday goods. Trading cards form a smaller part of its range. For this category, purchase costs can make a familiar retail price unviable; buying a larger quantity may reduce unit cost but tie up cash in slow-moving stock.

The project brings product identity, retail evidence, VAT, supplier price breaks and demand assumptions into one review. Its purpose is to decide what deserves further research or a controlled trial—not to treat every discount as an opportunity.

My contribution

Shop Assistant, September 2019–present. My work includes supplier communication, purchasing support, stock monitoring, customer service and financial administration. I independently selected 60 products for a wholesale application and developed an Excel tool covering more than 100 products.

This wider procurement project extends that practical work into Excel modelling and project-based SQL, Python and reporting development. It is not a claim that the entire technical pipeline was used operationally in the shop.

THE CENTRAL FINDING

Coverage is not the same as purchasing readiness.

222 of 240 products have an exact benchmark, but only 119 have an in-stock selected reference. The remaining 103 priced products need availability review; 18 still need price research.

01 · Establish comparability

Match the exact SKU, language, format and unit basis. Retain source IDs and observation dates. Similar items and future-dated references must not quietly become exact benchmarks.

02 · Test the economics

Compare shelf revenue excluding VAT with purchase cost and landed extras. Separate gross margin from markup and payment-fee contribution. Price ratings describe the offer’s economics, not supplier reliability.

03 · Check the commitment

Respect MOQ and order multiples. Evaluate bulk cash including VAT and stock cover against estimated demand. Review missing inputs, stock availability and unconfirmed terms before committing cash.

What the project does—and what remains unproven

Completed: a 240-product workbook, retained source evidence, price and bulk assessments, input controls and a reproducible SQL/Python reconciliation pipeline.

Confirmed personal scope: eight approved wholesale accounts and an independently selected 60-product application, as reported for the CV. These are distinct from the workbook’s 240-product scope and have not been independently audited against supplier correspondence here.

Not established: realised savings, increased sales, forecast accuracy or deployment of the reporting pipeline in daily shop operations. Estimated monthly demand is an assumption. A future approved trial would require actual sell-through and returns data to measure performance.

Evidence provenance and methodological limits

Real project: the scale, architecture, formulas and source logic below were inspected against the retained workbook and package. The pipeline was rerun privately on 15 October 2026.

Public methods: source code and aggregate structural evidence contain no commercial product rows. No renamed real prices or reversible anonymisation is used.

Synthetic demonstration: all 12 sample products, prices and demand values are fictional. The separate supplier example contains six fictional offers. These are not the 240-product dataset.

Important model difference: the original model retains exact non-future references and flags freshness separately; the demonstration removes observations older than 30 days before selection. The original supplier catalogue is not a comprehensive multi-supplier quotation database.

Actual workbook formulas

Read-only formula excerpts preserve the original references without exposing input values.

Excel · VAT-aware gross margin
=IF(A5="","",IF(OR(G5="",G5<=0,NOT(ISNUMBER(Q5))),"",1-(Q5+S5)/(G5/(1+Controls!$B$5))))

Shelf revenue is divided by 1 + VAT before deducting base cost and landed extras.

Excel · Minimum quantity and order multiples
=IF(A5="","",IF(OR(ISBLANK(Products!H5),R5="",AQ5<=0),"",ROUNDUP(MAX(Products!H5,AP5)/AQ5,0)*AQ5))

The bulk quantity respects the larger of the price-break threshold and MOQ, then rounds up to the order multiple.

Excel · Stock cover
=IF(A5="","",IF(OR(J5="",AN5="",AN5<=0),"",(AO5+J5)/AN5))

On-hand stock plus bulk units is divided by estimated monthly sales. Missing or non-positive demand leaves the result blank.

Excel · Maximum buying cost
=IF(A5="","",IF(G5="","",G5/(1+Controls!$B$5)*(1-Controls!$B$6)-S5))

The target-margin limit is calculated from net revenue, after allowing for landed extras.

SQL · Comparable supplier offers
-- Fixed basket: 24 units of each SKU. Fictional offers, excluding freight.
-- Offers requiring more units are excluded; a low price alone is not comparable.
WITH eligible AS (
 SELECT o.SKU,p.Product,s.Supplier,o.UnitCost,o.MinUnits,
        o.UnitCost*24 AS NetGoodsCost,
        DENSE_RANK() OVER(PARTITION BY o.SKU ORDER BY o.UnitCost) AS PriceRank
 FROM Offers o
 JOIN Products p ON p.SKU=o.SKU
 JOIN Suppliers s ON s.SupplierID=o.SupplierID
 WHERE o.MinUnits<=24
)
SELECT * FROM eligible ORDER BY SKU,PriceRank,Supplier;
Python · CSV ingestion into typed SQLite tables
with (ROOT/'data'/f'{table}.csv').open(newline='',encoding='utf-8') as f:
    rows=csv.DictReader(f); fields=rows.fieldnames
    db.executemany(f'INSERT INTO {table} VALUES ({",".join("?" for _ in fields)})',
                  [[r[k] if r[k]!='' else None for k in fields] for r in rows])
DAX · Filter-aware benchmark coverage
Priced Products = COUNT(Metrics[Benchmark])

Benchmark Coverage = DIVIDE([Priced Products], [Products Loaded])

04 / MODEL JUDGEMENT

Make the assumptions visible.

Evidence before economics

Only exact, non-future observations within 30 days qualify. In-stock listings take priority. A sold-out reference can support comparison, but still triggers review.

Margin is not markup

Margin uses revenue excluding VAT as its denominator. Payment fees reduce contribution. Bulk cash and stock cover show the commitment behind a unit discount.

Demonstration, not an outcome claim

These results use fictional fixtures. They do not establish realised savings, sales growth or forecast accuracy. The model is designed to make decisions explainable.

Verification boundary

SQL and Python checks have run. Excel formulas were recalculated and reconciled in the generation environment, including an input-change test. Native Excel and Power BI Desktop execution have not been performed. Power BI files are a model implementation pack, not a completed PBIX dashboard.
