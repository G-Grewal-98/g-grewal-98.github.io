01 / THE CASE

From product prices
to purchasing decisions.

This project grew from retail work comparing supplier offers, customer demand and selling prices. A discount alone did not answer the purchasing question: pack quantities, tax, availability and stock exposure also mattered.

The public edition isolates that decision process in a reproducible sample. It demonstrates how commercial assumptions become calculations, how source records become comparable benchmarks, and how exceptions remain visible.

QUESTION

Which offers meet the target margin, and when is bulk purchasing justified?

APPROACH

Filter and rank price evidence, calculate VAT-aware economics, then check availability and stock cover.

OUTPUT

A reviewable shortlist with reasons, supported by Excel formulas and independently checked SQL calculations.

CONTEXT & CONTRIBUTION

Commercial experience behind the model.

Workplace contribution

My retail role involves supplier communication, purchasing support and product evaluation. I independently selected 60 products for a wholesale application and developed an Excel tool covering more than 100 products to compare prices, margins and stock requirements.

The wider project contains a 240-product purchasing workbook. This public edition uses 12 fictional products and 24 retail-price observations. A separate six-offer example compares two fictional suppliers for three products.

Technical development

The portfolio extends that commercial problem through Excel, SQL, Python and reporting implementation files. It is project-based technical development, not a claim of paid analyst or software-engineering employment. The source and validation outputs are available for inspection.

SQL · Comparable supplier offers
-- Fixed basket: 24 units of each SKU. Fictional offers, excluding freight.
-- Offers requiring more units are excluded; a low price alone is not comparable.
WITH eligible AS (
 SELECT o.SKU,p.Product,s.Supplier,o.UnitCost,o.MinUnits,
        o.UnitCost*24 AS NetGoodsCost,
        DENSE_RANK() OVER(PARTITION BY o.SKU ORDER BY o.UnitCost) AS PriceRank
 FROM Offers o
 JOIN Products p ON p.SKU=o.SKU
 JOIN Suppliers s ON s.SupplierID=o.SupplierID
 WHERE o.MinUnits<=24
)
SELECT * FROM eligible ORDER BY SKU,PriceRank,Supplier;
Python · CSV ingestion into typed SQLite tables
with (ROOT/'data'/f'{table}.csv').open(newline='',encoding='utf-8') as f:
    rows=csv.DictReader(f); fields=rows.fieldnames
    db.executemany(f'INSERT INTO {table} VALUES ({",".join("?" for _ in fields)})',
                  [[r[k] if r[k]!='' else None for k in fields] for r in rows])

04 / MODEL JUDGEMENT

Make the assumptions visible.

Evidence before economics

Only exact, non-future observations within 30 days qualify. In-stock listings take priority. A sold-out reference can support comparison, but still triggers review.

Margin is not markup

Margin uses revenue excluding VAT as its denominator. Payment fees reduce contribution. Bulk cash and stock cover show the commitment behind a unit discount.

Demonstration, not an outcome claim

These results use fictional fixtures. They do not establish realised savings, sales growth or forecast accuracy. The model is designed to make decisions explainable.

Verification boundary

SQL and Python checks have run. Excel formulas were recalculated and reconciled in the generation environment, including an input-change test. Native Excel and Power BI Desktop execution have not been performed. Power BI files are a model implementation pack, not a completed PBIX dashboard.
